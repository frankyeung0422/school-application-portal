### 🧬 王琦 9-Constitution Typing — and why MDs should care

A short primer.

**What it is**: A classification framework developed by Prof. Wang Qi (Beijing University of Chinese Medicine), formally adopted by the China Association of Chinese Medicine in 2009 as a standard. It identifies 9 constitutional types:

1. **平和質** (Balanced) — what we'd call constitutionally healthy
2. **氣虛質** (Qi-deficient) — easily fatigued, low immune resilience
3. **陽虛質** (Yang-deficient) — cold-intolerant, low metabolic baseline
4. **陰虛質** (Yin-deficient) — heat-prone, dry, irritable
5. **痰濕質** (Phlegm-damp) — heaviness, metabolic-syndrome-ish presentation
6. **濕熱質** (Damp-heat) — acne-prone, bitter taste, oily skin
7. **血瘀質** (Blood-stasis) — sluggish circulation, fixed pain, dark complexion
8. **氣鬱質** (Qi-stagnation) — stress-pattern, mood-related somatic complaints
9. **特稟質** (Special constitution) — allergic / hereditary tendency

**Why it's not "vibes"**: It's a validated 60-item inventory (the [CCMI](https://example.example)) with documented test-retest reliability and external validity studies. Most TCM clinicians in mainland China are now required to document constitution typing at first visit; HK is following.

**Why a Western MD should care**:

1. **It's a tool for risk stratification you don't currently have.** A "陽虛質" patient is, in plain Western language, someone with low metabolic baseline + cold sensitivity + likely thyroid-borderline + likely Raynaud-positive + likely depressive-tendency. The constitution label captures a constellation of soft signals that don't yet add up to a diagnosis but predict treatment response. That has real clinical value.

2. **It explains the same-disease-different-response problem.** Why do two patients with identical hypertension respond differently to amlodipine? TCM says: their constitutions differ. Pharmacogenomics is starting to converge on the same insight from a different angle.

3. **It gives you a vocabulary for the "in-between" patient.** The patient who isn't sick by Western criteria but isn't well, the worried-well, the chronic-fatigued-not-yet-diagnosed. Constitution typing acknowledges and names this state instead of medicalizing it.

### 🔬 One herb-drug pair worth knowing this fortnight: 大蒜 × Saquinavir / Antiretrovirals

Last newsletter we covered 五味子 × Tacrolimus. This time:

**大蒜 (Garlic, supplement form)** lowers serum saquinavir levels by ~50% via CYP3A4 induction.

This matters because:
- HIV+ patients on protease-inhibitor regimens often take supplements without disclosing them.
- Garlic supplements are perceived as "just food" and rarely show up on med-rec lists.
- Subtherapeutic saquinavir levels → viral rebound → resistance.

**Clinical move**: For any patient on antiretrovirals, ask explicitly about garlic supplements — not just "any herbs?" The answer changes when you name the herb.

Culinary garlic in food: not a concern.
