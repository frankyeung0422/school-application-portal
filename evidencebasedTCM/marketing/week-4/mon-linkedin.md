**What worked**

→ The Monday build-in-public posts outperformed the Thursday education posts 3:1 by saved/shared. People care about the *journey* more than the *expertise*. I had this backwards.

→ The free interaction checker is the conversion funnel. Most of the demo bookings came from someone trying the public tool first, then DMing. Free tools beat free content.

→ Pricing publicly (HK$280/mo solo, HK$680/mo clinic) eliminated the awkward "what does it cost?" conversation. Demos became "let me show you what HK$280 buys".

**What didn't work**

→ Cross-posting LinkedIn content to other platforms. Wasted hours, generated near-zero traction. Sahil Lavingia is right: pick one platform.

→ Trying to demo to "anyone interested". The 2 of my 5 demos who said no were demographically wrong from the start (a med student, and a practitioner whose brand identity rejects external evidence frameworks). Should have qualified harder.

→ Writing thoughtful 1,000-word LinkedIn posts. The 200-word ones with a specific number outperformed every long-form attempt.

**What I'd cut if I started over**

→ The Substack newsletter. Two editions in, the email list is [N] subscribers. Useful, but it didn't drive demos. If I were starting again I'd skip this for the first 60 days and just send the cheat sheet as a one-shot landing-page download with no recurring commitment.

**What's next (60-day target)**

→ 10 paying customers
→ 1 clinic-tier subscription
→ First case study published (with the customer who closed in Week 3)
→ Apply for Cyberport / HKSTP healthcare incubator — having paying customers makes this conversation different.

## Notes for posting

- **Numbers must be real**. Don't round up. Don't omit the embarrassing ones. The "what didn't work" section is the credibility anchor — soft-pedaling it kills the whole post.
- **The "what I'd cut" line is the spiciest move**. Telling the Substack subscribers you'd cut Substack is risky-looking, but it's the move that gets re-shared. Audacious honesty is the only differentiator at this stage.
- **Don't make the "what's next" sound like a pivot**. The platform is the platform. The marketing changes; the product doesn't.
- **The Cyberport / HKSTP line is a strategic seed** — investors, accelerator partners, and potential clinic customers all read this. Costs you nothing to plant it.
