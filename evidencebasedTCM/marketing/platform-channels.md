# Channel-specific posting rules

## LinkedIn

**Length sweet spot**: 1,200–1,800 characters (about 200–300 words). LinkedIn truncates around 200 chars with "...see more". The first 2 lines must hook.

**Formatting that works**:
- Line breaks every 1–2 sentences. Mobile users scroll past walls of text.
- One emoji max in the hook line. None in the body.
- End with a question, not a CTA dump.
- Carousels (10-slide PDFs) outperform plain text 3–5× when the topic is reference material.

**Hook templates that work for clinical content**:
- "I read X papers so you don't have to. Here's what surprised me:"
- "Most [X] is [Y]. That's not a failure — here's why."
- "[Bold counterintuitive claim]. The evidence:"
- "A [practitioner type] asked me this last week. I didn't have a good answer until I [X]."

**Things to never post on LinkedIn**:
- "Excited to announce..."
- Generic motivational quotes
- Cross-posts from Twitter (different audience, different register)
- Anything that reads like a press release

## Substack

**Length**: 800–1,500 words per newsletter. Two sections — main piece + "this week on the platform" + question.

**Subject lines that work** (test in Substack analytics):
- "[Specific number] + [topic]" — e.g., "707 papers later"
- Direct first-person — e.g., "I demoed EBTCM to 5 practitioners. 3 said yes."
- Counterintuitive — e.g., "Why I'm not building an AI scribe"

**Formatting**:
- Lead with the punchline (don't bury the lead).
- Use **bold pull-quotes** every 3-4 paragraphs to break reading rhythm.
- One image, ideally a screenshot or a chart you made yourself (no stock).
- End with: (a) "Reply to this email with [specific question]" and (b) one link to the platform.

**Subscribers ≠ readers**. Open rate is the metric — aim for 35%+. Click-through to platform secondary.

## Image rules (both platforms)

- **Don't use stock photos of healthcare workers**. They scream "ad". Use real screenshots of the platform, hand-drawn diagrams, or simple Canva templates.
- **Carousel slides**: 1080×1080 (square). Light background, dark text. Same template across the series for brand consistency.
- **Hero images**: 1200×627 (LinkedIn share-link size). For Substack, 1456×816.
- **Color palette to stay on-brand**:
  - Primary: `#10b981` (emerald — clinical, evidence-y)
  - Severity: `#dc2626` (major), `#ea580c` (moderate), `#ca8a04` (minor)
  - Neutral: `#f8fafc` (bg), `#0f172a` (text)
- Always include the URL `evidencebasedtcm.com` small in the corner of every image. Free distribution.

## Cross-posting rule

LinkedIn primary, never the same exact copy elsewhere. If you must mirror to Twitter/X, rewrite for the platform (Twitter wants thread-like delivery, LinkedIn wants the post body intact).

Substack newsletter content can be expanded into a LinkedIn carousel 1 week later — but rewrite, don't paste.
