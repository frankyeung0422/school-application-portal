## Post body (copy-paste ready)

Two years ago, a friend's mother nearly bled out.

She'd been on warfarin for atrial fibrillation. Her TCM practitioner prescribed 丹參 (Salvia miltiorrhiza) for "circulatory health." Neither doctor knew about the other.

Her INR went to 8.6.

She lived. But that week I went looking for a tool that would have caught it before the Rx was signed.

There wasn't one. Not in English. Not in Chinese. Not free.

So I built EvidenceBasedTCM — a clinical decision support platform for the integrative-medicine reality that already exists in every Hong Kong clinic, whether the systems acknowledge it or not.

What's in it today:
↳ 220+ herbs with pregnancy / lactation / paediatric safety flags
↳ 707 GRADE-rated evidence entries (39 Grade A, 308 Grade B)
↳ Public herb-drug interaction checker — no login, no paywall
↳ Patient records with both 四診 (four-exam) AND SOAP modes
↳ Automatic interaction warnings when a prescription is saved against a patient's Western med list

What's NOT in it: paying customers. Zero, today. I'm in the middle of a 30-day sprint to get the first ten by demoing to practitioners one by one.

I'm going to document the sprint here in public. Wins, the harder no's, and what the conversations reveal that I couldn't see from inside the code.

If you're a TCM practitioner or integrative-med MD in HK / SEA / diaspora — try the free interaction checker and tell me what's wrong with it. The link is in the comments. I read every reply.

What's the most clinically uncomfortable herb-drug combination you've seen in practice?
