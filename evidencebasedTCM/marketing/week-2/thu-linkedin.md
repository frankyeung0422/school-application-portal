## Post body (copy-paste ready)

A patient walks into a TCM clinic and an FM clinic with the same complaint. Two completely different notes are written. Both are correct.

Here's the side-by-side I made for myself when I started bridging these worlds:

**The patient**: 47F, 2 weeks of headaches, worse with stress, neck tension, irregular sleep, takes amlodipine for HTN.

**The TCM note (四診)**:
- **問 (Inquiry)**: HA 2/52, frontotemporal, ↑ with stress, ↓ with rest. Sleep onset 1–2h. Bowels normal.
- **望 (Inspection)**: Slight facial flush, otherwise unremarkable.
- **聞 (Auscultation)**: Voice strong, no abnormal sounds.
- **切 (Palpation)**: Tongue — red tip, thin yellow coat. Pulse — left guan wiry, right cun-guan wiry-slippery.
- **辨證 (Pattern)**: 肝陽上亢 (Liver Yang Rising)
- **治法 (Principle)**: Subdue liver yang, soothe stress
- **方 (Rx)**: 天麻鈎藤飲 modified

**The Western (SOAP) note**:
- **S**: 47F c/o 2/52 HA, tension-type pattern, stress-aggravated, sleep onset insomnia. PMH: HTN on amlodipine 5 mg.
- **O**: BP 138/86, HR 78. No focal neuro deficit. Neck — trapezius tenderness, no meningismus.
- **A**: Tension-type headache + comorbid insomnia. Consider stress-related contribution. HTN sub-optimally controlled.
- **P**: NSAID PRN, sleep hygiene counselling, BP recheck 2 weeks, refer to CBT if persists.

**Both notes are valid**. They're operating on the same physiological reality with different frameworks. The TCM pattern names a relationship (liver yang rising due to stress). The Western dx names a mechanism (cervicogenic + tension component).

**The integrative move**: a tool that lets the same practitioner write either — or both — for the same patient. That's what we built into EBTCM's consultation form. One toggle, two frames, one chart that an integrative MD can hand to a TCM colleague for parallel care.

Three things this side-by-side has taught me:

1. The "TCM is vague / Western is precise" stereotype dissolves when you actually write both notes. The 四診 is just as structured as SOAP. The naming is different, not the rigour.

2. The single sentence that needs to be in **both** notes: "**Patient is on amlodipine 5 mg.**" Most TCM intakes still don't ask. Most SOAP notes don't ask what supplements the patient is taking. That's where the harm happens.

3. The patient doesn't care which framework is "right". She cares that her HA went away.

If you're an MD with TCM-curious patients, or a TCM practitioner with patients on chronic Western meds — what's the one piece of info you wish the other side always captured?
