### 🔬 One herb-drug pair worth knowing this week: 五味子 × Tacrolimus

If you have a transplant patient who takes traditional Chinese formulae:

**五味子 (Schisandra)** is a CYP3A4 **inhibitor** — it raises serum tacrolimus levels.

This is the inverse of the more famous St John's wort × tacrolimus interaction (where SJW *lowers* tacrolimus, risking graft rejection). Both are dangerous, but for opposite reasons.

Real cases in literature have reported 2–3× tacrolimus level spikes within a week of starting 五味子-containing formulae like 生脈散 or 五味子膠囊.

**The clinical move**: If your transplant patient is on tacrolimus, ask explicitly about 中藥 use. If they're taking anything 五味子-containing, get a trough level within 7 days. Do not trust the assumption that "herbs are mild."
